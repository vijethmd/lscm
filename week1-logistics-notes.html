Lecture 01S1–S13

Introduction to Logistics: Design and Management

Covers: definition, logistics evolution, key elements of logistics management, conclusion, books S2

What logistics is

Definition · exact words
"Logistics refers to the management of the flow of goods, information, and resources from the point of origin to the point of consumption." S7
  • The flow is forward as well as reverse. S7
  • It covers planning, implementing and controlling the efficient movement and storage of goods, plus the related information and financial transactions, within a supply chain. S7
  • Hook used in class: your latest online purchase arrives with one click; logistics is the "behind-the-scenes magic" that gets it there. S6

The Seven R's 7 items

Best practices in logistics management S8:

  1. Right product (including the right information about it)
  2. (At) right quantity
  3. Right time
  4. Right condition
  5. Right place
  6. (to) the right customer
  7. (with the) right (financial) resources

Local→Regional→National→Global S5

Key elements of logistics 5 items

Transportation · warehousing · inventory control · order fulfillment · distribution S10

Ultimate aim · exact words
"…to deliver the correct products to the right location at the appropriate time, all while minimizing expenses and maximizing effectiveness." S10

  • Conclusion: effective logistics optimizes operations, reduces costs, improves customer satisfaction, and is indispensable for business competitive advantage. S11

Logistics design and management

Suppliers→Plants→Port of Loading→Port of Destination→Warehouses→Customers
  • Strategic planning, coordination and execution of supply chain processes for an efficient flow of goods, information and resources from origin to consumption. S23
  • Includes inventory management, warehousing, transportation, order processing and distribution. S23
  • Good design weighs cost optimization, customer satisfaction, sustainability and risk management. S23
  • Involves analysing demand patterns, choosing transport modes, designing warehouse layouts, inventory control systems, and real-time tracking. It needs continuous evaluation and improvement. S23

Lecture 04S66–S89

Storage and Warehousing

Covers: storage, warehousing, cross docking (utilities, challenges, impacts), case studies S67

Storage · exact words
"Storage in logistics and supply chain management refers to the process of storing goods, materials, and products at various points along the supply chain, from raw materials procurement to finished goods distribution." S68

Good storage management keeps materials flowing, optimizes inventory and meets demand. S68

  • Blinkit, Zepto and other 10-minute deliveries come from dark stores, which shows why storage matters. S69
  • The storage diagram links five "rights" (time, price, place, products, customers) to store operations, customer, category, supplier, logistics & warehouse, and forecasting/replenishment/promotions management, around optimized business processes and an integrated operations database. S70

Components of storage

WarehousingStorage hubs before distribution; owned by manufacturers, wholesalers, retailers or 3PLs; shelving, racks, pallets. S71
Inventory managementEnough stock, low holding cost: optimization, replenishment, cycle counting, valuation. S71
Storage facilitiesWarehouses, distribution centres, cold storage (perishables), bonded warehouses (goods under customs control), cross-docking facilities (immediate transfer without storage). S71
Storage equipmentPallet racks, shelving, mezzanine floors, forklifts, conveyors, AS/RS (automated storage and retrieval systems). S72
Safety & securityCameras, access control, alarms, barriers; safety protocols prevent accidents. S72
Material handlingMoving, protecting, storing and controlling materials from manufacturing to distribution. Manual (physical force) vs automated (robots, computerized devices). S72

Warehousing

Warehouse · exact words
"A warehouse is a commercial facility designed for the storage of goods, materials, and products." S74

A centralized hub where items are stored temporarily before distribution; uses shelving, racks, pallets and inventory systems. S74

Warehouse layout design: FAST

F · FlowStock inflow and outflow strategy. S75
A · AccessibilityEnough room for staff and forklifts to move safely and quickly. S75
S · SpaceMaximum space for operational storage and stock processing; minimum for offices, monitoring, empty pallet storage. S75
T · ThroughputUnits processed per time interval; usually measures outbound operations. S75

7 steps to plan the layout S76

  1. Define the overall purpose of the warehouse
  2. Create a warehouse diagram and calculate the total storage area
  3. Consider storage requirements
  4. Evaluate material handling equipment needs
  5. Map it out and optimize space
  6. Test traffic flow
  7. Implement the layout
Common layoutBest for S76
U shapeThe most common; works with any type of warehouse.
I shapeThrough-flow; high volumes; separates loading and shipping areas.
L shapeLots of storage needs; greater sorting areas.

Utilities of warehouses 7 items

Product consolidationCombine goods from many suppliers into larger shipments; lower transport cost. S78
Cross-dockingUnload, sort and load straight onto outbound trucks; little or no storage. S78
Value-added servicesPackaging, labelling, kitting, assembly. Kitting = packing complementary products into one package sold as a "kit". S78
Seasonal storageHold seasonal or excess stock for peaks; avoid stock-outs and overstock. S79
Inventory managementStore until needed for production or distribution. S79
Strategic locationNear customers or transport hubs; lower cost, faster delivery. S79
Risk mitigationBuffer against supplier delays, transport issues, natural disasters. S79

Case studies

AmazonFast-delivery retail model. Opened what it called its first inbound cross-docking distribution centre in Germany: receives from suppliers and distributes across Europe within a day; robots work alongside humans. S84
Roche Diagnostics (Switzerland)Pharma; mostly perishable products needing same-day delivery. Uses cross-docking for temperature control and fast delivery. S85
Mainfreight (New Zealand)Offers cross-docking services in NZ and worldwide: consolidate freight, cut warehousing cost, save time, fewer bottlenecks. Best-suited products: perishables, retail products with constant demand, pre-barcoded ready-for-sale goods. S86

Conclusion: effective storage management gives a smooth material flow, optimized inventory and met demand. The warehouse is for temporary storage at a strategic location and sometimes adds value. S87

Lecture 05S90–S127

Logistics Services Providers

Covers: LSP introduction, key components, outsourcing logistics, 1PL to 5PL, case study S91

Logistics service provider (LSP) · exact words
"A logistics service provider (LSP) is a company or organization that offers a range of logistical services to support the transportation, storage, distribution, and management of goods and materials for businesses." S92

Key components of LSPs 8 items

Services offeredTransport (road, rail, air, sea), warehousing, freight forwarding, customs brokerage, inventory, fulfilment, packaging, consulting, technology. S94
CustomizationTailored by industry, location, volume, handling needs, budget. S94
Expertise & experienceSpecialists in transport, warehousing, customs compliance, inventory optimization. S95
Global reachGlobal presence or partner networks. S95
Technology integrationTMS, WMS, GPS, RFID, cloud platforms. S96
Risk managementInsurance, contingency planning, regulatory compliance. S96
Cost efficiencyEconomies of scale; pricing such as pay-per-use or volume-based. S97
Continuous improvementTrack metrics, gather feedback, invest in tech and process upgrades. S97

Outsourcing logistics: industry trends

  • A.T. Kearney survey, why companies outsource (in the order shown, tallest bar first): avoid capital expenditures, cut costs, increase flexibility (service/financial), focus on core business, improve service, lack of scale, acquire talent, avoid labour problems. S99
  • Most outsourced: domestic transportation 83%, warehousing 66%, international transportation 63%, customs brokerage 46%, freight forwarding 46%. Least: fleet management 10%, customer service 11%, LLP/4PL services 11%. S100
  • Activities that are more strategic and customer-facing are outsourced less often. S100

1PL to 5PL at a glance

TypeWho / whatAsset-based?Eggs example S101
1PLFirm or individual with its own cargo that transports it; the consigner. Two parties (supplier and retailer/customer), no middlemen. S102Own assetsA farmer who delivers eggs to a grocery store to sell
2PLAsset-based carrier that owns the means of transport (rail, road, sea, air). E.g. airlines, shipping lines, hauling companies. Used for international transport of heavy and wholesale goods. S105YesA courier delivers eggs from farm to grocery store
3PLTransport and delivery plus warehousing, terminal operations, customs brokerage, SCM, IT for tracking. S108UsuallyA fulfilment company with a fleet of trucks transports eggs
4PLOverseer managing an entire supply chain; called Lead Logistics Provider; like a consultant; contracts many 3PLs; single invoice; no physical means to move inventory. S114NoA company that manages a 3PL on the farmer's behalf
5PLLogistics aggregator; bundles demand of 3PLs into bulk to get better rates from airlines and shipping companies; one step above a 4PL. S120NoA company that manages the farmer's supply chain from production to delivery

1PL components

  • Ownership and control: the company runs its own transport, warehousing, distribution and inventory. S103
  • In-house operations: own fleet, warehouses, staff, tech. S103
  • Vertical integration with manufacturing, procurement and sales. S103
  • Benefits: control over quality, security, customer service. Challenges: costly fleets and warehouses, hard to scale. Risk: the company carries all of it. S104

2PL components

  • Narrower service scope than 3PLs; may specialize in transport, warehousing, freight forwarding, customs brokerage or fulfilment. S106
  • Specialized expertise (e.g. a transport 2PL with trucks, drivers and a TMS); customized solutions. S106
  • Integration with client operations; scalability and flexibility without long-term commitments; technology (TMS, WMS, tracking). S107

3PL components

  • 90% of Fortune 500 companies use a 3PL (Armstrong & Associates). Flow: Manufacturer → [packaging, warehousing, transportation] → Market. S109
  • Services pie: transportation management 22%, warehousing 21%, value added 19%, integrated 9%, international 9%, private fleets 7%, other 5%, lead logistics 4%, intermodal 4%. S108
  • Example, Hitachi Transport System: procurement, production, sales and reverse logistics services, plus collaborative logistics within the same industry. S110
  • Components: comprehensive services, outsourced expertise (focus on core competencies), scalability and flexibility S111; global reach, technology integration S112; cost savings, risk management S113.

4PL components

  • Integration and coordination of multiple 3PLs and partners; the 4PL is the orchestrator. S116
  • End-to-end visibility: consolidates data from 3PLs, ERP systems and IoT devices. S116
  • Customized solutions; technology integration (TMS, WMS, analytics, cloud) S117; risk management; continuous improvement S118; global reach; strategic insights and planning S119.

5PL and beyond

  • Not asset-based; works across all disciplines; a fully integrated solution covering the whole chain through multiple outsourced providers. S120

Case study: Elixia and a leading Indian 3PL

  • Client: a leading Indian 3PL covering 1,00,000+ km/month; inbound, outbound, primary and secondary distribution, last-mile delivery. S123
  • Problem: improper vehicle allocation, vehicles running at incomplete capacity, inefficient route planning for local and upcountry deliveries. S124
  • Result: clear budgeted costs and vehicle needs; INR 6.7 lac saved in one month by optimizing load, space and route. S124
  • Headline figures: 8.4% cost savings after route and space optimization · 650 Tn carbon savings · 25% increase in vehicle space utilization. S125

Memory hook (my own, not from the slides)

Climb the PL ladder by how much of the chain is handed over. 1PL does it yourself. 2PL hires a truck. 3PL hires a truck company that also stores and packs. 4PL hires a manager who runs several 3PLs (one invoice, no trucks). 5PL pools many 3PLs' demand to bargain for bulk rates.
