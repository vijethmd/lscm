Lecture 37S1–S30

Strategic Sourcing

Strategic sourcing S3

A procurement strategy for identifying, evaluating and selecting suppliers on specific criteria to optimize the supply chain and meet long-term goals. It goes beyond finding the lowest-cost supplier: build strong relationships, improve quality and delivery, reduce cost and risk, and align sourcing with organizational strategy.

Characteristics 7 items

Disciplined buying practices · total cost focus (total cost of ownership, not just price) · consolidated purchasing power · tight supplier relationships · improved teamwork and purchasing skills · realigned business processes · continuous improvement · focus on value creation. S4–S6

Supplier selection and management

  • Suppliers matter for meeting requirements, controlling unit cost, cutting total acquisition cost, relying on fewer suppliers, and designing subassemblies. Suppliers are treated as partners. S7
  • Selection criteria: price, quality, delivery, performance history, warranties and claims, capacity, technical capability, financial position. S8
  • Supplier certification: certified suppliers have proven they consistently deliver quality, which reduces routine testing. S8

Supplier Relationship Management (SRM)

  • Collaborative, mutually beneficial relationships with key suppliers based on trust and a win-win orientation. Objectives include becoming the "customer of choice", leveraging supplier capabilities for innovation, sharing growth, profit, risk and investment. S10
  • Setting up SRM: define the strategy → identify key suppliers → assess capabilities → governance structure (cross-functional) → collaboration initiatives → invest in technology → open communication → performance measurement → supplier development programs. S11–S13

  • Segmentation criteria: spend, risk, criticality, performance, market position, geographic location. S17
  • ABC analysis (Pareto 80/20): A = few suppliers, most spend; B = moderate; C = many suppliers, low spend. S18

Global sourcing: eight dimensions

Cost (total landed cost) · quality · CSR · risk · opportunities · politics · local context · enabling infrastructure. S20–S21

Case: Colorado manufacturer

  • Founded 1950s; build-to-order, low-volume high-mix; outdated MRP → about $1M excess and obsolete inventory; customer lead time 12 weeks, double competitors'. S22–S23
  • New President (Lean expert) spent the first 100 days assessing; consultant partly funded by the Rocky Mountain Trade Adjustment Assistance Center; cross-functional team. S23
  • Seven steps: define objectives (cut lead time 80% to two weeks) → specify critical parts (machined parts; total landed cost; local suppliers) → assess suppliers (NDA, questionnaire, 3-year price stability) → select and allocate → award to five shops (10–30% each), Kanban → implement (blanket PO, new ERP, daily buyer walk) → track (quarterly scorecard). Lecture 38S31–S51

Vendor Rating

  • Vendor rating: a formal evaluation system that scores suppliers on criteria such as delivery, quality, price and service; often a weighted formula of quality and delivery ratings. Important where large sums are involved, e.g. railways. S33
  • Four core criteria: quality, price, delivery, service; also reliability, innovation, relationship. S34–S36
  • Need: performance evaluation, supplier development, risk mitigation, cost optimization, benchmarking, supplier relationship management. S37–S38

8 steps S39–S41: define criteria → assign weights → gather data → evaluate performance → calculate ratings (e.g. weighted scoring) → review and validate → communicate and give feedback → use for decisions.

Techniques

Categorical planManagers list factors from experience and grade vendors good, satisfactory, poor; very subjective. S43
Weighted point planFactors are weighted and vendors scored on each. S43
Cost ratio planCost of quality, price, delivery etc. as a % of total purchase value. Example: delivery cost Rs 5,000 on purchases of Rs 1,00,000. S44
Forced decision matrixAttributes compared in pairs: the more important gets 1, the other 0. S45
Service cost ratioSubjective intangibles: labour stability, financial stability, flexibility for rush orders, R&D. S45
IBM quality ratingVGR = desired cost of inspection ÷ actual cost of inspection × 100. S45
Bell quality ratingBell Helicopter's Lot Quality Index: LQI = X ÷ L, with X = L1×1.00 + L2×2.10 + L3×2.90 + L4×3.10 + L5×3.90. S46
  • Bell lots: L1 accepted as received; L2 rejected by sampling but labelled; L3 rejected, reworked at supplier; L4 rejected, returned not usable; L5 rejected, reworked at Bell. L = total lots received. S46
  • The technique chart also lists "Eavaston's vendor selection". S42
  • Advantages: less subjectivity, feedback from all areas in one package, better communication, overall control of the vendor base, specific corrective action, continuous review standards. S48
Lecture 39S52–S77

Supply Chain Coordination

  • Coordination: strategic alignment of processes, activities and information among suppliers, manufacturers, distributors and retailers to optimize the whole chain. Key elements: demand forecasting, inventory, production planning, transport, communication. S54
  • With suppliers: collaborative forecasting, information sharing, VMI, JIT delivery, quality standards, regular meetings, integrated IT. S55–S56
  • With distributors: efficient order processing, distribution planning, stock replenishment, safety stock, reliable delivery, returns, sales data sharing, KPIs, joint business planning. S57–S58
  • Coordination points between suppliers and distributors: purchase orders, production plan, inventory management, delivery and transportation. S59–S60
  • Lack of coordination causes the bullwhip effect. Causes: forecasting errors, order batching, price fluctuations, lack of information sharing. Effects: higher costs, excess inventory, stockouts, poor service. S61 S63
Performance measureImpact of lack of coordination S64

Obstacles to coordination

Incentive (inventive) obstacles · information processing obstacles · operational obstacles · pricing obstacles · behavioural obstacles (lack of trust). S62

Managerial actions 5 groups

1. Align goals and incentivesMaximize total SC surplus; pricing for coordination (lot-size quantity discounts, two-part tariffs, buyback, revenue-sharing, quantity flexibility); sales force incentives from sell-in to sell-through. S65–S66
2. Information visibility and accuracyShare POS data; collaborative forecasting; single-stage control of replenishment (CRP, VMI). S67
3. Operational performanceReduce replenishment lead time; reduce lot sizes; ration based on past sales to limit gaming. S68
4. Pricing to stabilize ordersMove from lot-size-based to volume-based discounts; EDLP; tie promotions to sell-through. S69
5. Strategic partnerships and trustTrust and information sharing; clear contracts and conflict resolution. S70
  • Macro processes: CRM, ISCM, SRM. S71
  • Amazon CRM: personalized recommendations, one-click ordering, order visibility, call-back option. 2008 CRM market (Gartner): SAP 22.5%, Oracle 16.1%, Salesforce.com 10.6%, Microsoft 6.4%, Amdocs 4.9%. S72–S73
  • Hindustan Oil Company (HOC): efficient SCM, quality, R&D, sustainability. S74
Lecture 40S78–S98

Supply Chain Performance: Balanced Scorecard

  • Measures: qualitative (customer satisfaction, product quality) and quantitative (order-to-delivery lead time, response time, flexibility, resource utilization). Quantitative splits into financial and non-financial. S80–S81
  • Financial measures: raw material cost, revenue, holding cost, transport cost, activity-based costs, expired perishables, penalties and credits for late or wrong orders, returns. S82
  • Non-financial: cycle time (supply chain lead time; order-to-delivery lead time), customer service level (order fill rate, stockout rate = its reverse, backorder level, probability of on-time delivery), inventory (raw, WIP, spares, finished), resource utilization (manufacturing, storage, logistics, human, financial). S83–S85
  • Qualitative: satisfaction, complaints, response time, quality, delivery experience, order accuracy. S86
Balanced Scorecard S87

Developed by Robert Kaplan and David Norton. Combines financial and non-financial indicators across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Business Processes, Learning and Growth, aligning activities with vision and strategy.

CustomerCost, service, quality, time: "Number One in delivering value to customers". S89
Internal processActivities per function, bottlenecks, alignment, automation. S90
Learning & growthJob satisfaction, turnover, training, right expertise. S91
FinancialROI, cash flow, quarterly/annual results, ROCE. S92
  • Benefits: aligns measures with strategy at all levels, turns the mission into daily "marching orders"; used by nearly 60% of Fortune 500 companies. S95
  • Pitfalls: no well-defined strategy, too much focus on lagging measures, generic copied metrics, self-serving managers. S96
Lecture 41S99–S130

Balanced Scorecard (contd.): QDC Indices and Techniques

  • SC BSC: Finance (revenue, gross margin, ROI), Clients (satisfaction, loyalty), Internal (machine productivity, response times), Improvement & innovation (training, new products). S101–S104
  • Procurement BSC: financial (savings, TCO, cost per transaction), customer (complaints, internal satisfaction), internal processes (suppliers for 90% of spend, lead time from requisition to order), learning (training, certification, turnover). S105
  • Implementing: define objectives → develop metrics → collect and analyse data → review and adjust → foster collaboration. S106

Slide quirks to watch

  • Cost ratio example: Rs 5,000 ÷ Rs 1,00,000 × 100 = 5%, but the slide prints "10%". S44
  • Inventory turns and DOS formulas are garbled on the slide ("Annualized COGS × Average Inventory Investment", "Inventory Investment × Daily COGS"). The usual forms are turns = annual COGS ÷ average inventory and DOS = inventory ÷ daily COGS. S114
  • "% Non-compliance rectified" divides by "total number of non-compliance rectified"; the denominator should be total non-compliances. S109
  • "Inventive obstacles" in the coordination slide is Chopra's "incentive obstacles". S62
  • "Eavaston's vendor selection" appears in the technique chart but is never explained; the "Vendor Rating Format" slide (S47) is blank in the PDF.
  • The Lecture 37 title slide reads "37: Strategic Sourcing / Lecture –". S1
