Lecture 06S1–S28

E-Logistics

Covers: introduction, the e-commerce scenario, a same-day delivery case, challenges, key elements S2

E-logistics · exact words
"E-logistics is defined as the management of all the physical flows of an organisation that sells goods on an online platform (website, marketplace, etc.)." S3
  • Also called electronic logistics or digital logistics: digital tools and systems used to manage and optimize logistics. S3
  • It is set against traditional retailer logistics, but the two can be complementary. S3
  • The withdrawal (return) period is much longer online, so e-merchants need a returns management policy. S4
  • Fevad: 35% of customers with a bad delivery experience will not order from that site again. S4

Solutions that matter to customers

  • Several delivery methods: home delivery is most popular; pick-up points (Relay Point) are popular in France; express D+1 delivery is almost unavoidable. Greener delivery: Generix found 87%+ of French people interested, so bicycles and EVs for the last mile help stand out. S11
  • Free delivery: PayPal/ComScore found 43% of French online buyers abandoned a basket over high shipping costs. Alternatives: a minimum purchase amount, free delivery on the next order, or free delivery for a limited period. S12
  • Delivery tracking: almost obligatory; reassures customers and saves customer-service time. S12

Challenges: returns

  • At least 30% of online orders are returned vs 8.89% in brick-and-mortar stores. 92% of consumers will buy again if returns are easy; 79% want free return shipping; 67% check the returns page first; 62% are more likely to shop online if they can return in-store; 49% of retailers offer free return shipping. S17
  • Return reasons: wrong item 23%, product looks different 22%, damaged 20%, other 35%. S17
  • Sample Amazon returns dashboard: ordered quantity 181,027, total returns 1,343, return rate 0.74%. S18

Case: the same-day delivery race

Amazon's game planWarehouses in densely populated urban areas, inventory closer to customers. S19
Walmart's countermovePilot programs in select cities using its store network; partners with UPS; flat $10 fee, no minimum order. S20
eBay's creative twistSame-day delivery for items not in its own inventory, via local retailers and dedicated couriers. S21
  • Asia Pacific same-day delivery market by type (B2B, B2C, C2C): 905.7 (2016) → 1,119.0 (2017) USD million, rising to 2027. S22
  • Global same-day delivery: expected growth 18.8% through 2027, market size $15.8 bn by 2027. S23
  • Why same-day matters: 49% more likely to shop online if available; 51% of 18–34-year-olds expect it; 99% of retailers plan to offer it by 2024; it raises competitive edge by 85%. S24
  • Quick commerce in India: Blinkit 39%, Swiggy Instamart 37%, Zepto 20%, others 4%; market about $700 million, expected $5.5 bn by 2025. S25
  • What customers order (LocalCircles): last-minute essentials 37%, all groceries 29%, both 23%, indulgence items 11%. S26
Lecture 07S29–S54

International Logistics

Covers: international logistics, key components, cross-border transactions (types, steps, challenges), major stakeholders S30

International logistics · exact words
"…international logistics is the study, planning and implementation of how a business moves physical goods and materials from supplier to customer in a way that involves crossing at least one international border." S31

It also includes the international movement of money and information: business logistics spanning two or more countries. S31

Main goals 3 items

Cost efficiencyUsually the first goal: multiple tax regimes, tariffs, long-distance shipping. S32
ReliabilityOne misunderstood local rule can make a whole shipment unusable (confiscated, perishables turned away). S33
RelationshipsReliable, fair-priced delivery builds loyalty; can be the one competitive advantage for low-differentiation products. S33

Key components 9 items

Customs clearanceCommercial invoices, packing lists, certificates of origin, customs declarations; brokers or forwarders help. S35
Supply chain visibilityGPS, RFID, IoT sensors for real-time tracking. S35
Inventory managementSafety stock, demand forecasting, supply chain segmentation. S36
Risk managementGeopolitics, currency, trade barriers, disasters; diversify suppliers and routes, insurance. S36
IncotermsInternational Commercial Terms: define delivery point, transport responsibility, transfer of risk between buyer and seller. S37
Cross-border regulationsTrade compliance, tariff classification, export controls, licensing. S37
Cultural & language considerationsCommunication, cultural sensitivity, business etiquette. S38
Global supply chain networkCoordination among partners across countries. S38
SustainabilityGreen transport, eco-friendly packaging, energy-efficient operations. S39

Cross-border transactions

  • A financial transaction where the payer and recipient are in separate countries. Types: retail, wholesale, B2C, B2B. S40
  • Value forecast to exceed $250 trillion by 2027, up over $100 trillion in a decade. S40
  • Growth drivers: international trade and e-commerce; manufacturers' cross-border supply chains; global investment; remote workers paid across borders; an international workforce sending money home. S41

7 steps S42–S44

  1. Purchase or initiation using local payment methods
  2. Routing and processing via an encrypted gateway
  3. Payment verification by the receiving bank (funds after conversion and fees)
  4. Transaction confirmation (or a "declined" message)
  5. Transaction fulfilment: merchant processes the order
  6. Settlement through a global automated clearing house (ACH), typically 2–5 business days
  7. Tracking: each bank sends a reconciliation report

Cross-border payments can take weeks to reconcile and cost up to ten times a domestic transaction. S45 Challenges listed:

High funding costsCapital set aside in advance in the right currencies. S45
Transaction feesMore entities, more bank fees. S46
Currency exchange ratesRates move between initiation and settlement. S46
Long transaction chainsEach "link" adds time, fees, checks and data-corruption risk; multi-currency chains are longer. S46
Tax and legal implicationsE.g. sales tax thresholds differ by country. S47
Limited operating hoursSettlement depends on banks' hours in both locations. S47
Fragmented, truncated data formatsDifferent standards; translation causes delays. S48
Legacy technology platformsBatch processing, no real-time monitoring. S48
Complex compliance checksFraud, sanctions, financial-crime checks; false flags on similar names. S49

Major stakeholders in international consignments 10 items

ExporterSeller or shipper; makes a sale to a foreign country. S51
ImporterThe buyer purchasing from a foreign country. S51
Freight forwarderPlans, coordinates and monitors all transport operations, including auxiliary services. S51
Customs agentAuthorized to do customs clearance formalities on others' behalf: documents, inspections, duties. S51
Shipping companyLegal owner of the vessel(s). S51
Shipping agentRepresentative of the shipping company at a given port. S52
ConsolidatorSells cubic metres of container space; groups goods from various exporters to fill a container. Used by small-volume shippers. S52
CarrierMoves the container from a location to the destination port. S52
InsuranceCovers incidents in transit for a premium. S52
BanksFinancial side, e.g. the letter of credit. S52

The six strategies 6 items

Large players

  • Worldwide: XPO Logistics, UPS, NFI, DHL (Deutsche Post), Ryder, C.H. Robinson, DB Schenker, J.B. Hunt, Expeditors, FedEx. S81
  • India: Lokesh Logistics, Blue Dart, Gati, FedEx, Mahindra Logistics, Safexpress, Gati-KWE, Allcargo, Delhivery, Ecom Express. S82

Case: syringes for Peru's COVID-19 vaccination drive

  • Challenge: regular syringe shipments from a factory in China to Peru amid pandemic congestion and flight restrictions. S83
  • Strategy: Noatum Logistics Peru found the only viable route, 48 hours from the Shanghai factory. S83
  • Solution: monthly charter flights, passenger area used for cargo; 8 air charters and part charters via Shanghai – Alaska – Los Angeles – Mexico – Lima. Peru's Foreign Ministry and Embassy in China helped get slots at Shanghai airport. S84
  • Result: syringes arrived on time every month at reasonable cost. S84

Logistics Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

  • Measuring logistics performance means assessing efficiency, effectiveness and compliance with goals. KPIs include on-time delivery, order accuracy, inventory turnover, transport cost, warehouse utilization, customer satisfaction. S89
  • Common KPIs: shipping time, order accuracy, picking accuracy, delivery time, pick & pack cycle time, order cycle time, equipment and trailer utilization, transportation costs, dwell time (time goods spend at a location), inventory carrying cost, warehousing cost, pick & pack cost, operating ratio (operating expenses vs revenue), packing material use, number of shipments, inventory accuracy, inventory turnover, inventory-to-sales ratio. S91–S93

Lecture 10S115–S125

Tools for Improving Logistics Efficiency

  • Quality in logistics = conformity to requirements and consistency in meeting standards. S117
  • Quality assurance (QA) prevents mistakes and defects. Two principles: Fit for purpose and Right first time. S117
  • Meaning of quality: the producer's perspective (production → quality of conformance: conformance to specifications, cost) and the consumer's perspective (marketing → quality of design: quality characteristics, price) both lead to fitness for consumer use. S118
Deming's costs of qualityExamples S119
Cost of conformanceQuality training, studies, surveys
Cost of non-conformanceRework, scrap, inventory costs, warranty costs

Problem-solving techniques: PDCA, 7 QC tools, quality circles, 5S, benchmarking. S122

PDCA (plan–do–check–act)

  • Also called the continuous improvement spiral or Deming wheel; drawn as a wheel because it is cyclical. S123
  • Made popular by William Edwards Deming; its creation is attributed to his mentor Walter Shewhart. S123
PlanDefine the problem. Three sub-processes: identify the opportunity and analyse the start; set measurable targets; prepare an action plan. S123
DoThe test phase: perform the action, remove the causes. S124
CheckEvaluate results vs plan (often with data visualizations). Goal met → move to Act. Not met because the plan was poorly applied → go back and review deployment. S124
Act (adjust)Document, standardize and monitor the change. S124
  • In logistics, quality means meeting demand at the lowest cost without compromising service level. S125
  • Quality costs: spent to obtain quality, e.g. a preventive maintenance plan. Non-quality costs: from lack of quality, e.g. downtime losses. S125

Tools for Improving Logistics Efficiency (contd.)

Covers: 7 QC tools, quality circles, 5S, benchmarking, conclusion S127

The seven QC tools

  • Introduced by Kaoru Ishikawa in Guide to Quality Control (1976); these simple tools can solve 90% of quality problems. S128
ToolPurpose · slide case
1. FlowchartShows steps and sequence; finds bottlenecks. Case: electronics assembly redesigned, training, quality checks, real-time monitoring. S129–S130
2. Check sheetEasiest and fastest way to collect data as tally marks; mainly frequency data for histograms. Case: defects in electronic component assembly. S131–S134
3. HistogramShows variation (dispersion) and averages; classes on the horizontal axis, frequency on the vertical. Case: restaurant delivery times; the 25–30 min peak shrank, most orders moved to 15–20 min. S135–S137
4. Pareto diagram80-20 rule: 80% of problems from 20% of causes; Ishikawa calls it the "first step to making improvements"; shows which problem to tackle first. Case: scratched screens (50) and battery issues (30) prioritized. S139–S141
5. Cause-and-effectAlso fishbone or Ishikawa diagram; finds root causes by category. Case: restaurant satisfaction; root causes understaffing and inefficient workflow. S142–S144
6. Scatter diagramRelationship between two variables: positive, negative or no correlation. Case: more training programs → better job performance (positive). S145–S148
7. Control chartInvented by Walter A. Shewhart in the 1920s (Shewhart chart); centre line = mean; upper and lower control limits at ±3 standard deviations. Inside limits = common-cause variation (no action); outside = special-cause variation (investigate). S149–S152

Quality circles

  • Kaoru Ishikawa is considered the father of quality circles. S154
  • A voluntary group doing the same or similar work in the same area; meets one hour a week in normal working time; solves work problems at no or marginal extra cost. S154
  • Size: ideally 8–10 members; never fewer than 5 or more than 15. S154
Top managementShow support, give budget, set up awards, promote healthy competition. S155
Steering committeeSenior managers: framework, objectives, policy, nominate coordinators and facilitators, decide rewards. S155
CoordinatorRuns QC activity organization-wide; registers all circles; documentation; training material. S156
FacilitatorSenior officer of the department; arranges training and resources; link between circles and management. S156
LeaderRotates; starts as a supervisor; convenes meetings, sets goals, delegates, builds consensus. S157
MembersFrom the work area; punctual, learn statistical tools, raise problems, contribute ideas. S157

5S philosophy

Five Japanese terms for waste reduction and an organized workplace. S158

Seiri · SortKeep only essential items.
Seiton · Set in orderEvery item has a designated place.
Seiso · ShineKeep areas clean and orderly.
Seiketsu · StandardizeStandards for organization and processes.
Shitsuke · SustainKeep practices going; audits for discipline.

Benchmarking

  • A systematic process of measuring, comparing and improving performance against industry leaders or best-in-class companies. S159
  • Six steps: define objectives → identify KPIs → gather data → select partners → analyse and compare data → identify opportunities. S160
Conclusion: seven quality concepts S161

Customer focus · continuous improvement (never-ending) · employee empowerment · use of quality tools · product design · process management (build quality into the process) · managing supplier quality.
